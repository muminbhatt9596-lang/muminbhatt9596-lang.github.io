Start here

Five rules decide whether a beginner makes progress. Swipe through them, then pick a plan.

Turn up

Three sessions a week you actually finish beat six you plan and skip. Pick days and times you can keep.

Form first

Use light weights for the first two weeks. Slow, controlled reps teach your body the movement and protect your joints.

Add a little

Progress means one more rep or a slightly heavier weight than last time. Write your numbers down.

Eat and sleep

Muscle is built during recovery. Get enough protein every day and 7 to 9 hours of sleep.

Be patient

Expect real changes in 8 to 12 weeks. Photos and notes show progress the mirror hides.

How to read the plans

  • 3 x 10 means 3 sets of 10 reps, with rest between sets.
  • 8-10 reps: choose a weight where you reach 8 with about 2 left in the tank. Hit 10 on every set, then add weight.
  • Rest: 90 seconds for big lifts, 60 seconds for smaller ones.
  • Tempo: lower for 2 to 3 seconds, then lift with control. No bouncing.
  • Warm-up (5 to 8 min): easy cardio, arm circles, cat-cow, 10 squats, 10 glute bridges, then a light first set.

Progression: how to keep improving

WeekFocus
1Learn form. Do 2 sets of each exercise with light weights (RPE 6).
2Add the third set. Keep weights the same.
3Add reps until you reach the top of each rep range.
4When you hit the top reps on every set, add the smallest weight jump (about 2.5 kg for dumbbell and cable lifts).
5Easy week: about two thirds of the sets. Then repeat weeks 2 to 4 with heavier weights.

Exercise guide

Hover or tap a card to flip it. Front: the move. Back: the cue to remember and the mistake to avoid.

Goblet squat

Legs, glutes
Cue: chest up, sit between your hips, knees track over toes.
Avoid: heels lifting off the floor.

Romanian deadlift

Hamstrings, glutes
Cue: push hips back, keep the weight close to your legs.
Avoid: rounding the lower back.

Dumbbell bench press

Chest, triceps
Cue: shoulder blades back and down, elbows about 45 degrees from the body.
Avoid: flaring elbows straight out.

Push-up

Chest, shoulders, core
Cue: one straight line from head to heels, chest to the floor.
Avoid: sagging hips.

Seated cable row

Back, biceps
Cue: pull elbows toward your hips, pause for one second.
Avoid: leaning back to swing the weight.

Lat pulldown

Lats, biceps
Cue: pull to the upper chest with elbows down.
Avoid: pulling behind the neck.

Overhead press

Shoulders, triceps
Cue: squeeze glutes, ribs down, press straight up.
Avoid: arching the lower back.

Reverse lunge

Legs, glutes
Cue: step back, front shin stays vertical.
Avoid: short steps that push the knee forward.

Glute bridge

Glutes
Cue: chin tucked, pause and squeeze at the top.
Avoid: arching the back instead of using the hips.

Plank

Core
Cue: ribs down, squeeze glutes, push the floor away.
Avoid: hips too high or too low.

Dead bug

Deep core
Cue: keep your lower back flat on the floor.
Avoid: moving too fast.

Face pull

Rear shoulders, upper back
Cue: pull to your face, elbows high, hands apart at the end.
Avoid: using too much weight.

Fuel the work

Keep it simple. Eat enough protein, eat mostly whole foods, and adjust calories to your goal.

  • Protein: about 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg of bodyweight a day, over 3 to 4 meals.
  • Calories: lose fat at about 300 to 500 below maintenance. Build muscle at about 200 to 300 above.
  • Water: 2 to 3 litres a day, more when you sweat.
  • Sleep: 7 to 9 hours. Poor sleep raises hunger and lowers training quality.

Common questions

How many days a week should I train?

Three full-body days is enough to make strong progress. Four days works if you recover well. More is not better at the start.

Will lifting make me bulky?

No. Gaining large amounts of muscle takes years of hard training and a calorie surplus. Beginners get stronger and leaner first.

How long until I see results?

Strength usually goes up within 2 to 3 weeks. Visible changes take about 8 to 12 weeks of steady training and eating.

I missed a workout. What now?

Do the next workout in order. Do not double up. One missed session changes nothing, one missed month does.

Do I need supplements?

No. Food comes first. If you struggle to reach your protein, whey protein is a convenient, well-studied option.

Is muscle soreness normal?

Mild soreness for 1 to 2 days in the first weeks is normal. Sharp, joint or lasting pain is not. Lower the weight and see a professional if it continues.

Should I do cardio?

Yes, in small amounts. Walk daily and add 1 to 2 easy sessions of 20 to 30 minutes for heart health. Keep lifting as the main focus.
